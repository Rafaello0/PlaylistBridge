Privacy Policy

Updated: November 11, 2026 · PlaylistBridge Windows desktop application

PlaylistBridge is an independent Windows desktop application that helps users transfer playlist entries between Spotify and YouTube. It is not affiliated with Google, YouTube, or Spotify. This policy describes what information the application accesses, processes, stores and discloses, including data provided by YouTube API Services. It also explains what is saved to your Windows device and how to delete it.

1. Services, authorization and user choice

PlaylistBridge uses YouTube API Services, including the YouTube Data API v3, and Spotify's official APIs. You choose which accounts to connect, which playlists to read, which search results to select, and when to create or modify destination playlists. PlaylistBridge does not download or transfer the underlying audiovisual files.

For YouTube, the application requests the Google OAuth scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl, which allows reading and managing YouTube account resources, such as playlists and their entries, within Google's authorization rules. You review and grant or refuse this access using Google's own OAuth screen. Spotify permissions are requested through Spotify's own authorization process.

2. Categories of information accessed and stored

  • Google/YouTube account information: the selected authorized YouTube channel name and channel ID, and account/channel identifiers returned by the API.
  • YouTube playlists and videos: playlist IDs, titles, links, playlist items and their video IDs, video titles, search results, candidate matches and other related API metadata needed to choose and update playlists. These may concern your own authorized playlists or publicly searchable content.
  • Spotify information: playlist and track IDs, names, artists, album information, saved tracks, playlist links and track metadata required to select source content and find corresponding results.
  • Authentication information: locally saved OAuth access/refresh tokens, authorization scopes and expiration information for the connected providers. PlaylistBridge never asks you to enter your Google or Spotify password into the application.
  • Local application information: settings, chosen playlist links, source/destination associations, full candidate lists, transfer progress, recovery sessions, timestamps and diagnostic logs. Error logs may contain playlist/video titles, IDs, URLs and error responses relevant to troubleshooting.

The application does not request your Google account password. It does not independently collect contact lists, phone contacts, unrelated browsing history or location data for playlist transfers.

3. Purposes and processing of information

  • Identify the YouTube channel selected during OAuth authorization and display the relevant channel information.
  • Retrieve authorized playlists, their entries and available destination playlists.
  • Use Spotify track titles and artist names in YouTube search queries to locate potential video matches.
  • Use YouTube playlist/video IDs and metadata to display matching options and detect whether a video already appears in a selected destination.
  • Send playlist creation or modification requests to the appropriate provider only when you initiate the corresponding action.
  • Remember user choices, avoid unnecessary repeated API requests, and resume an interrupted transfer within applicable retention limits.
  • Keep local diagnostic information for troubleshooting and technical support.

4. Recipients and sharing with external services

PlaylistBridge processes playlist data locally and communicates directly from your Windows computer with Google's OAuth services, YouTube API Services and Spotify's APIs over encrypted HTTPS connections. It does not operate a publisher-hosted account database or music-library backend.

  • Google/YouTube: receives OAuth requests, playlist API requests, track titles and artist names included in YouTube searches, selected video IDs, and requested changes to your YouTube playlists. Google and YouTube process those requests under their own terms and privacy practices.
  • Spotify: receives Spotify authorization requests, source-playlist and music metadata requests, and playlist changes you initiate for a Spotify destination.
  • System browser and OAuth providers: your browser opens Google and Spotify authorization pages. The providers can process connection data and browser cookies during sign-in.
  • Developer / internal access: the developer does not automatically receive your OAuth tokens, playlists or local transfer database. Information is only provided to the developer when you intentionally contact support and share logs, screenshots or other details. Any such information is used to address your support request.

PlaylistBridge does not sell YouTube API Data or user information, share it with data brokers, use it for targeted advertising, or integrate independent advertising or behavior-tracking analytics in the Windows application.

5. Local device storage, cookies and similar technologies

PlaylistBridge writes files to %LOCALAPPDATA%\PlaylistBridge within your Windows account. These may include settings.json, encrypted files in tokens, match-cache.json, target-playlists.json, target-track-index.json, last-session.json, and diagnostic logs. These files allow the application to remember settings, sessions, OAuth authorization, search choices and transfer information. Sensitive saved credentials are protected using the Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI) for the current user profile.

The Windows application does not set its own browser cookies or use advertising identifiers. It opens Google and Spotify authentication pages in your system browser; Google, Spotify and the browser may place, read, recognize or retain cookies and similar storage technologies as part of sign-in, account security and provider functionality. PlaylistBridge does not control their cookie practices. Refer to the linked provider privacy policies and your browser settings.

This public website is hosted on GitHub Pages. Visiting it involves connections to GitHub's hosting infrastructure and may involve browser storage or server access logs under GitHub's policies. The desktop application and the documentation website are separate environments.

6. Retention and refresh of YouTube API Data

Locally saved YouTube search results, playlist/video metadata, candidate matches, playlist associations, transfer-recovery snapshots and indexed records are limited to 30 calendar days from the relevant observation or saved-data timestamp, unless they are refreshed from current API information. Stored data that is no longer current must be refreshed or removed before being reused. The application checks stored records when they are read, removes expired records during application use and at startup, and periodically cleans up while it remains running. The application is not a background Windows service, so when it is completely closed, file cleanup takes place on its next start.

OAuth authorization tokens may be retained for as long as they are required for an active authorized connection and consistent with the user's consent. The application's account-disconnection functions remove related local data. Local diagnostic logs are also subject to retention cleanup and may be deleted as whole files rather than retained indefinitely.

Because YouTube content and permissions can change independently of PlaylistBridge, the application rechecks relevant current API information when required for transfer or duplicate prevention. YouTube API Data is not intended to be presented as currently authoritative after its allowed cache age has expired.

7. Your controls, revocation and deletion

You can request removal of locally stored YouTube information by choosing Disconnect YouTube within PlaylistBridge. When remote revocation succeeds or the token is already invalid, the app removes the saved YouTube authorization token and related local search, session, association, index and diagnostic data. If Google cannot be reached, you can choose local-only removal and revoke the application's permission yourself through Google Account third-party connections. Cleanup interrupted by a crash is resumed the next time the application starts.

You can disconnect Spotify separately. You can also remove all PlaylistBridge local data from your Windows user profile after closing the application. For data-deletion questions or requests, contact fanmobilea@gmail.com. Requests concerning data under the developer's control are handled as promptly as possible and within seven calendar days.

Deleting local PlaylistBridge information does not delete playlists, videos or other information hosted by YouTube or Spotify. To delete remote content, use the corresponding official service or a supported authorized application action.

8. Security

PlaylistBridge uses provider-hosted OAuth sign-in, HTTPS API calls and DPAPI to protect eligible locally saved credentials. No storage or transfer technology eliminates all risk. Do not send passwords, OAuth tokens or recovery codes to the developer.

9. Third-party terms and privacy notices

PlaylistBridge uses YouTube API Services. By using the YouTube functionality, you are also subject to the YouTube Terms of Service. See the Google Privacy Policy, Spotify Privacy Policy, and GitHub Privacy Statement.

10. Updates and contact

The developer may update this policy to reflect verified functionality or changes to the providers' requirements. Material changes are indicated by the update date above. Questions, requests for data deletion and privacy inquiries: fanmobilea@gmail.com.
